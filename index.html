Why Adrastea? Five things that set us apart.

Research-driven. Conducting original research, not just raising awareness or networking.

Women across the lifespan. Addressing women’s health at every age, not only among students.

Youth-led, built to scale. Bringing in younger fellows as today’s leaders move on.

Research made accessible. Translating findings through social media, not just paywalled publications.

A unique intersection. Women’s health and space medicine, approached through science, engineering, policy, and media.

Women’s health in space needs more than one kind of expertise. We don’t just identify the gaps. We build the research, projects, and partnerships that close them.

How our work comes together

1963 to today

How we got here.

Six decades of women in space, and of the research catching up with them.

1963

First woman in space

Valentina Tereshkova orbits Earth 48 times aboard Vostok 6.

1983

First American woman in space

Sally Ride flies on the Space Shuttle Challenger.

1992

First Black woman in space

Mae Jemison, physician and engineer, flies aboard Endeavour.

1993

Women written into research

A US law requires women to be included in NIH-funded clinical research.

1999

First woman to command a Shuttle

Eileen Collins leads a Space Shuttle mission.

2014

Sex and gender in space

NASA reviews how women’s and men’s bodies adapt to spaceflight, and how thin the data is.

2019

First all-woman spacewalk

Christina Koch and Jessica Meir step outside the International Space Station.

2022

Measuring radiation for women

Two phantom torsos modelled on the female body, Helga and Zohar, fly around the Moon on Artemis I.

2026

Adrastea launches

The next chapter is ours to write. Ad astra, for all.
